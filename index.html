Carlo's Bijbaan Scout

Every job below advertises a wage. Almost none of them advertise Carlo's. Dutch minimum wage is scaled by age, and eighteen is the worst rung on the ladder, so the number in the ad usually belongs to somebody older.

Two raises are already scheduled. The band rises on 1 January, and he turns 19 on 30 April. +50% by the end of April, arriving by law, nothing to negotiate. Any rate agreed now is worth revisiting on both dates, which is a reason not to lock into a long fixed arrangement.
Worth knowing

There is no earnings limit. DUO: "Vanaf 2020 mag u onbeperkt bijverdienen. Er geldt geen bijverdiengrens." Nothing is clawed back. But his timetable caps him near 16 hours a week, so the rate is the only thing that can move.

The one question

"Wat is het uurloon voor iemand van 18?"

Ask it in the first message, every time. It costs nothing, it filters out the €7.50 employers in one exchange, and it is an ordinary question in the Dutch market. The age scale is public law, not a negotiation. No posting below answers it, whatever its tag says.

Read the column literally

None of these tags tells you what Carlo gets paid. They describe the advertisement, which is the only thing that can be checked from outside.

“Says 21+ rate” is the one settled case: the employer has written down that the headline figure belongs to someone older. “Beats the minimum” is not the opposite of that. It means the ad pays over the adult floor and simply never mentions age, which is the ordinary case and no promise at all. Above the legal floor an employer may run an age scale or not, and nothing on the page can tell you which. Agency listings are the weakest of the lot: the rate often turns out to be the client’s own age-based scale, applied after placement.

The way around it

The minimum wage is a rule about employees. It does not reach the self-employed, so on a flex platform like Temper or YoungOnes, where Carlo would be a ZZP'er rather than staff, the youth band does not apply to him at all.

Temper's own floor is €16.00 an hour. That is 2.1× his employed floor, for the same shifts. He is 18, exactly the platform's minimum age, and Temper allows one shift with no VAT number, so trying it costs nothing but an evening.

What he gives up is real: no holiday pay, no sick pay, no pension, no unemployment cover. Regular work needs a KVK registration and a BTW number, though the small-business scheme removes the quarterly VAT filing under about €20k.

And the ground is moving under it

On 16 June 2026 the Amsterdam Court of Appeal ruled that Temper's workers are temp staff, not self-employed, reversing a 2024 decision. Temper took it to the Supreme Court in July and the ruling is suspended while that runs, so nothing has changed yet.

But temp-agency pay scales are themselves age-based. If it is upheld, the youth discount comes straight back. Use it, don't bet on it: a platform account and an employed bijbaan.

Where this actually gets won

Scraping boards is the narrow part. It catches well-paid postings quickly and refuses the floor consistently. But most Amsterdam student work never becomes a structured posting at all.

Do one Temper shift

The free trial shift needs no VAT number and no paperwork. It answers whether the €16 route is worth registering for.

Register, don't just browse

YoungCapital and StudentJob as a candidate rather than as a website. Recruiters surface roles before they post.

Go direct where the language pays

Hotels, museums, Schiphol, Italian restaurants. Amsterdam runs Italian-language desks year-round.
